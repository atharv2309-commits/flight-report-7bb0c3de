In short

Wind on the return flight was stronger than normal, gusting up to 16 metres per second (about 58 km/h). Flying against wind that strong uses far more battery than a calm flight does, and this flight burned through its charge much faster than expected on the way home.

Your pilot saw the picture from the drone start to break up and made the right call: bringing the aircraft home early rather than waiting. Around a minute later, the aircraft's own battery safety system reached the same conclusion independently, so the early call made no difference to the outcome. It kept flying home until its battery reserve ran low enough that the built in safety system stepped in and landed it immediately, rather than risk running out of power in the air. That landing happened close to the dock, but not quite at it.

Separately, the drone's main video and data signal dropped out almost completely for about 11 minutes in the middle of the flight, from 15:20 to 15:31. That is a real, longer outage and worth being upfront about. What kept the flight safe through it was the backup connection: it carried video, data, and full control of the aircraft for the entire 11 minutes, so the drone was never actually out of contact or out of control.
